# 15. Wallets that bump fees: results

Generated by `experiments/15-fee-bumping/run.py`, seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 30 nodes, 1800 simulated seconds a run. Do not edit by hand.

## 1. Wallets that bump

Experiment 00's network and demand: 30 nodes, 8 peers each, link latency 20 to 100 ms, a block every 12 s from a proposer that rotates through every node, gas limit 2100000 (100 transfers, a target of 50), base fee from 1 gwei; 400 senders of plain transfers at 2.5 a second from 0 to 600 s, 12.0 a second to 900 s and 2.5 a second to 1800 s, and 100 of frame transactions at 0.2 a second. Each sender's wallet sends to one node, drawn when it first sends, with a tip uniform in 0.001 to 3 gwei and a fee cap of twice the next base fee plus the tip. A wallet that bumps replaces its oldest pending transaction, or every one, after the given number of blocks its node imports without it, raising tip and fee cap by 10%, the least NP-11 admits, and to the market raises the fee cap further to twice the next base fee plus the new tip. Rows pool five runs, seeds 1 to 5. Experiment 00 sent each transfer to a random node, so its run and seed 1 with wallets that never bump are close but not the same.

Delays are in seconds from a user's asking to the block that included any version of its transfer, for transfers asked for in the burst and after it. Replacements and the gas price paid, in gwei, are means over the burst's transfers. Stuck senders are node n00's senders of plain transfers with a pending transaction whose fee cap is below the next base fee after the last block, as in experiment 00; they, the pool, which is pending transactions per node, and the base fee are at the end of the run, as a mean and the range over runs. Cleared is the runs in which the pool fell back to the most it held in the quiet phase, and the latest it did so, in seconds after the burst.

## 3. A shared sender under `one`

Section 1's runs with a shared sender as well, under `eip-8141`, whose users ask for a spend every 36 s on average until 1500 s, a third of the one spend a slot that `one` allows (experiment 01). Its relayer sends one spend at a time and the next once that one is included, so every user waits behind the spend that is pending. The spend's fees are committed by its user's signature entry, so only its user can re-price it, which it does as the second column says, and the relayer sends the new version in place of the old. Delays are from asking to inclusion, in seconds; replacements are per spend; the longest hold is the longest time between two of the sender's spends landing, or from the last to the end of the run if a spend never landed, as a mean and the range over runs.
