# 02. MATCHA against one pending transaction per sender: results

Generated by `experiments/02-matcha/run.py`, seed 1, 20 nodes, 4 simulated hours a run unless a section says otherwise. Do not edit by hand.

## The spend

The voucher-pool spend of an earlier design study: an expiry frame, the pool's `self_verify` frame, a `SENDER` frame calling a token, a 256-bit nullifier as its EIP-8250 nonce key at sequence 0, and one signature entry by the user. Every spend is the same apart from its key, its deadline, its tip and its fee cap. Admission gas and the charge are MA-2's; the included gas is what the sender earns as width when the block is final, here its whole `max_gas`. In the second table, what width sustains assumes every additional spend is charged once and lands, with one free baseline in every slot; the cap's ceiling is a baseline a slot plus the additional spends one cap of width pays for, because finality, and so width, arrives once an epoch of 32 slots.

Landed and demand are spends an hour, over the run and over its last two hours. Delays are seconds from the user's request to the block that included the spend. The first multi-spend block is when a block first carried more than one spend, and first width when n00 was first credited, both in minutes from the start. Re-simulations and work are per node per block: work is the admission gas of every prefix the pools ran for the sender, at admission or after a head change. ## 3. Mass invalidation

A malicious application `evil` is its own shared sender. Its relayer sends 600 paid spends an hour, at the honest tips, for the first 45 minutes, so that under MATCHA it earns width. From minute 60 to minute 80, after every block its node n05 imports, it sends up to 100 junk spends whose prefix reads a setting slot of `evil` and whose tip, 1 wei, is below every builder's minimum, and an account it controls sends a 26,000-gas transaction that changes the setting in the next block, invalidating every junk spend pending anywhere. Per block of the attack: junk admitted at n05, and per node the re-simulations and the work (as in section 1, for `evil`, in gas). The attacker's gas is what its flips used per block of the attack; its paid spends used the gas in the last column before it, under every policy. The ratio is work per node against the attacker's flip gas, over the attack; width is n05's for `evil` at its start and end.

## 4. Width draining

Section 1's honest pool at 1200 spends an hour, and for the last two hours an adversary holding vouchers for it: after every block its node n05 imports, it sends up to 20 junk spends from the honest pool, valid, with a fee cap over the next base fee and a tip of 1 wei, below every builder's minimum, to n05 alone or to every node. The rows with no adversary are section 1's runs. Landed is honest spends by hour of the run, so the adversary acts in hours 3 and 4; delay is over spends asked in those hours, and is n/a when none of them had landed by the end. Junk admitted counts the junk spends some target took; junk held is the mean number pending per node at the end. `disjoint-keys` is not run: there every junk spend is re-run at every node at every block, because each included honest spend moves the pool's balance.
