## 1. What one pool lets a never-included transaction push out

A pool at the pending base fee of 1 gwei is filled to capacity with honest transactions from distinct senders, priced as wallets price, a tip of 0.001 to 3 gwei and a fee cap of twice the base fee plus the tip: *pays*, without a deadline; *deadline*, the same with an expiry frame 600 s out (frame pool only); and *stranded*, a fee cap between half the base fee and just below it, as a backlog is after the base fee rises. They are in equal shares, a third each in the frame pool and half each in the account-nonce pool. Then as many again of the adversary's transactions arrive, one at a time, each from a sender of its own: *free*, a fee cap and tip of 1 wei; *sub-minimum*, a tip of 999999 wei, one below the builders' minimum, and a fee cap of twice the base fee plus the tip; *expiring*, a deadline at the head's timestamp and a tip of 10 gwei, so that it is valid now and cannot be in the next block. No builder includes any of them. Out is how many of each honest class are no longer held; the last column is the commonest answer to the adversary's transactions that were refused.

## 2. A flood on the network

Wallets that pay for themselves send 1500 frame transactions an hour to random nodes, half of them with a deadline 600 s after first sending; each wallet has one in flight, sends it again to a random node after every block that leaves it out, and gives it up once its deadline has passed. From minute 10 to minute 30 an adversary keeps every node's frame pool full of *free* transactions, sent to each node directly at the start and after every block it imports: enough to fill it and 16 more. Rows are the transactions first sent during the flood. Landed in the flood is before minute 30; delays are of the landed ones, in seconds from first sending; overflow is `ErrTxPoolOverflow` answers per transaction, and evicted is FP-14 evictions per transaction across nodes.
