# Experiment 24: proving time in the aggregation interval

Generated by `experiments/24-proving-budget/run.py`, seed 1. Costs from `bench/results/2026-10-13-leanvm.md` (decision records 0033 and 0034).

## 1. Against demand

A network of 30 nodes, 8 peers each, a block every 12 s from a rotating proposer, gas limit 30000000. Wallets, each its own default-code sender, send transactions with one dependency to random nodes for 15 minutes of a 20-minute run, each as a mode 0 wrapper with its proof; a wallet with one in flight waits, and after every block that leaves it out sends it again to a random node. A leanSTARK transaction takes 133849 gas of a block with AG-8's charge, a leanSPHINCS one 106849. Every node aggregates once a second at a phase of its own, `cap_aggregates` false except in the capped row, and sends its aggregate after its proving time (AG-9) plus the link's latency. Costs: `prove_base` 468.0 ms, `prove_sig` 19.9 ms a leanSPHINCS triple, `prove_recursive` and `prove_stark` 951.7 ms a recursive input or leanSTARK triple on this machine; on the laptop 79.1, 3.4 and 178.0 ms, this machine's scaled by the README's time over ours, 0.169 for its aggregation and 0.187 for its recursion. With a budget, a node lists in AG-7's order only what it can prove in 1000 ms; without one it lists everything and skips the ticks that come while it is still proving. Spread is the mean number of nodes a transaction became pending at; carried is the mean, over the slots of the demand, of the distinct transactions listed in any aggregate during the slot; time, inputs and direct are the mean proving time of an aggregate in ms, the recursive STARKs it took and the triples it verified directly; busy is the share of ticks skipped during the demand; a s is the aggregates a node made a second during the demand, and KiB/s what it sent its 8 peers at 319.9 KiB a proof, the benchmark's recursions, transactions not counted.

## 2. When only some nodes can prove

## 3. A flood that never pays

Section 1's leanSTARK transactions at 8 a slot, and from second 60 an adversary that, after every block a node imports, hands that node directly k frame transactions with one leanSTARK dependency each, as mode 0 wrappers, at a tip of 10000000000 wei, above every honest tip, each from a sender of its own and all paid by one default-code sponsor of that block's own; with them it hands the node the sponsor's transfer of its whole balance at twice that tip, which the next block includes first, so that every one of the k fails there and leaves every pool at the next head (experiment 05's `drained` flood). Junk share is the mean share of an aggregate's transactions that are the adversary's; sent counts its transactions handed to a node; included counts those in a block.
