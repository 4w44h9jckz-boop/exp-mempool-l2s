## The message, for the wallet

Fixed at `fdc4bb3`, with the scheme named at `cb18a37`
(`docs/spec/10-claim-gated-admission.md` version 6, CG-8, CG-10 and
CG-12).

**What the issuer signs.** The message, 152 bytes, in this order:

| Field | Bytes | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chain ID | 32 | big-endian |
| sender | 20 | |
| nonce keys | 32 | `nonce_keys_hash`: the keccak256 of the count of keys and each key, each as 32 bytes, big-endian, the word `TXPARAM(0x0F)` returns |
| nonce sequence | 8 | big-endian |
| epoch | 8 | big-endian |
| salt | 32 | |
| binder | 20 | the address of the buyer's one-time `secp256k1` key: the last 20 bytes of the keccak256 of its 64-byte uncompressed public key |

The first six fields are version 5's 132 bytes, unchanged. The binder is
appended last.

**Namespace and signature.** Unchanged. The issuer signs it, blind, as
Commonware's `ops::sign_message` for `MinSig` does under the namespace
`FRAME_TICKET_V1`:
- the hashed bytes are the namespace's length as a varint (15), the
  namespace, then the message;
- the hash to G1 uses the domain separation tag
  `BLS_SIG_BLS12381G1_XMD:SHA-256_SSWU_RO_POP_`;
- the issuer's key is in G2.

**The paymaster's frame data.** 188 bytes:
- the epoch, 8 bytes;
- the salt, 32 bytes;
- the binder, 20 bytes;
- the signature, 128 bytes, a G1 point in EIP-2537's encoding.

**What the one-time key signs.** EIP-8141's `compute_sig_hash(tx)`, the
word `TXPARAM(0x08)` returns. It goes in the transaction's `signatures` as
one entry:
- `scheme` 1 (`SECP256K1`);
- `signer` the binder's 20 bytes;
- `msg` empty;
- `signature` is v, then r, then s: v is 0 or 1, r and s are 32 bytes
  each, and s is low.

The protocol verifies the entry before any frame runs. The hash covers
everything but the bytes of signatures whose `msg` is empty: fees, frames,
the payer and the ticket's own frame included. EIP-8141 on master at
`25b19523` is the same here.

**What a bound template checks**, in `VERIFY`:
1. it rebuilds the 152-byte message from `CHAINID`, `TXPARAM` and its
   frame's data;
2. it checks the pairing;
3. it finds an entry whose `SIGPARAM` scheme is 1, whose `msg` is 0, and
   whose resolved signer is the binder.

**Its template.** When the template is deployed, its length and the
keccak256 of the code before the 256-byte key and 32-byte denomination go
into `claim-gated.json` (`CG-10`'s `bound_template_length` and
`bound_template_keccak`). They also go into skandha's
`--frames.boundTemplate <length>:<keccak256>`.

**A worked example.** `conformance/vectors/claim-gated/cg-12-bound-copies.json`,
case "the original", gives a whole transaction's bytes, its signature hash,
the binder and the ticket. `cg-08-ticket-message.json`, case "one key, with
a binder", gives a message.

## Learned

- **EIP-8141 already has the place for the binder's signature.** A
  `SECP256K1` entry of the transaction's own list may name any signer. The
  protocol checks it over the signature hash before any frame runs, and
  `SIGPARAM` shows a paymaster its signer and `msg`.
- **Frame data cannot carry it.** A signature over the hash cannot sit in
  frame data, which the hash covers.
- **An explicit-digest entry checks a node's recovery, not its order of
  steps.** Recovering over the signature hash refuses such an entry
  anyway. A node that trusts each entry's own `msg`, as the protocol
  validates it, accepts it: that is the break the case catches.
- **One thing leaves a bound claim bound:** the bytes of an `arbitrary`
  signature with an empty `msg`. They are outside the hash by the EIP's
  design.
