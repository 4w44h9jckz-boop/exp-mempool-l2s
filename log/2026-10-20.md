# 2026-10-20: item 49 opens, the ticket

## Done

- Item 49, first part: what a claim's `valid` flag means for a real
  transaction. `docs/spec/10-claim-gated-admission.md` version 3, on `base`
  at `9ace8a2`, adds CG-8, the 132 bytes an issuer signs (chain ID, sender,
  EIP-8250's `nonce_keys_hash`, sequence number, epoch, salt); CG-9, a
  BLS12-381 signature in G1 under an accepted key in G2, both in EIP-2537's
  encoding, verified as Commonware's `verify_message` for `MinSig` does,
  under the namespace `FRAME_TICKET_V1`; and CG-10, the ticket as the data
  of a `VERIFY` frame to a ticket paymaster the node accepts (decision
  record 0056).
- The format is the one a wallet had already run on the Hegota testnet,
  `exp-frames` experiment 36, with its namespace replaced. A first draft
  here, which listed the nonce keys one by one, was withdrawn unpublished.
- 61 vectors: signatures from Commonware at `c18a6b9c`, each valid one also
  issued blind and found equal; every verdict on decoding and the pairing
  from the testnet's EIP-2537 pairing precompile; four tickets the testnet
  mined, which verify over CG-8's message under the run's namespace and not
  under this one.
- `ref/python/emem/bls.py`, standard library only: RFC 9380's hash to G1,
  the isogeny's 53 constants parsed from the RFC's text and its five test
  vectors passing, both encodings, and the optimal ate pairing, about a
  second a verification. skandha (`packages/framepool`, on
  `@noble/curves`) plays all 61, and claim-gated admission with received
  wrappers: 448 vector cases pass there, none fail.

## Learned

- A verifier that leaves out G1's subgroup check accepts a valid signature
  plus $(0, p - 2)$, a point of order 3: the pairing check alone passes.
  One vector holds that case; a point outside G1 chosen at random fails the
  pairing anyway.
- `@noble/curves` 1.8.1 decodes a compressed point by reducing its
  coordinate modulo $p$, so a signature written with its $x$ plus $p$
  verifies there and not in Commonware. Its affine constructor refuses the
  same coordinate, so skandha decodes EIP-2537's encoding itself and lets
  noble build the point.
- The twist's group of points over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ is not cyclic in its
  13-part: every point times the order over 13 is the point at infinity,
  and a point of order 13 takes the order over $13^2$.
- A ticket paymaster's `VERIFY` frame spent 122,006 gas on the testnet,
  above `eip-8141`'s `max_verify_gas` of 100,000, and FP-11 lets it sponsor
  one pending transaction at a time.

## Open questions

- How a node learns which ticket paymasters to accept for an epoch. CG-10
  takes the set as given.
- A clause for a ticket paymaster's frame cost and its pending count, for a
  profile that admits ticket-paid transactions at the EIP's limits.
- EIP-8250 on master (`0ce679f4`) allows `nonce_keys == []`, which FP-3,
  read at `258836f9`, refuses.

# 2026-10-20: item 49, a ticket from a real transaction

## Done

- `10-claim-gated-admission.md` version 4, on `base` at `15a0692`: a ticket
  paymaster is any account whose code is the 1,105-byte template a wallet
  deployed on the Hegota testnet, a key in G2 and a 32-byte denomination,
  with no list and no registry (CG-10, decision record 0057). CG-9's issuer
  is the key in that code. The vectors were written again; two of CG-10's
  are spends the testnet mined, with their paymasters' code.
- `11-surface.md` version 3, on `base` at `10fd022`: SF-3 reads the claim
  with the code the execution client answers at the head for each `VERIFY`
  frame's target, and SF-4 runs CG-2, CG-4 and CG-6 before it asks the
  client (decision record 0058). Eleven surface cases are transactions from
  the testnet, at the head the spend was built on: one mined spend, the
  three the testnet refused, and two spends sent at once.
- skandha reads the code with `eth_getCode` under `--frames.claims`, keeps
  the paymasters it finds, and plays all 466 vector cases it can (266 need
  pools it does not have). Run against the testnet with no configuration
  but the flag, it refused the three junk tickets with `ErrClaimInvalid`
  without asking its client, and read the mined ticket as valid
  (skandha `2b1b4b0`, `docs/frames/runs/2026-10-20-tickets/`).
- `make experiments` on `10fd022`'s tree was stopped at the session's
  one-hour limit for a background command before it printed. Nothing it
  runs can move: `before_prefix` is called only by the surface, and no
  experiment imports the surface or the replay. It is running again with a
  two-hour limit; its outcome goes in the next entry.

## Learned

- The ticket paymaster is not the canonical paymaster, so FP-11 holds it to
  one pending transaction; the testnet's client refused the second of two
  valid spends sent at once, and so does the reference. Every sender behind
  one ticket paymaster shares one pending place per node.
- Without the check before the client, the testnet's client spends the whole
  prefix on a junk ticket, the paymaster's pairing included, and answers
  `validation prefix frame reverted`.

## Open questions

- A rule for ticket paymasters in FP-11, or many paymasters per issuer, if
  a crowd is to have more than one spend pending at once.
- What would price a self-issuer's junk proofs (decision record 0057): a
  memory of the paymasters under whose tickets a proof failed.

# 2026-10-20: item 50, a self-issuer's junk proofs and two defences

## Done

- The pull request from `base` to `main` is open, as asked:
  [4w44h9jckz-boop/exp-mempool-l2s#2](https://github.com/4w44h9jckz-boop/exp-mempool-l2s/pull/2).
  Its CI ran every experiment at `10fd022`, each in a job of its own, and
  all 25 passed with `verify`. Locally `make experiments` cannot finish
  within the session's two-hour limit for a background command on four
  cores, and was stopped each time before it printed.
- `base` at `a47d650`, which joins that pull request: the simulator models two defences against a
  self-issuer's junk proofs, issuer memory and a node list, around the
  clauses and not as clauses (`sim/emem_sim/issuers.py`, decision record
  0059), with five tests.
- `exp/25-ticket-defences` at `b636953`: experiment 25 prices version 4
  as written, issuer memory with $k$ = 1 and 8, and node lists, in node
  work, in the attacker's spend and in honest senders' delay. Its README
  separates what was measured (on the testnet, in a wallet's run and in
  the leanVM benchmark) from what the simulator models.

## Learned

- Under version 4 one deployment buys a self-issuer unlimited junk proofs:
  128 a node an epoch at 4 wrappers a slot, about 3 gas of deployment a
  proof over a day.
- Issuer memory caps the junk per paymaster, per node and per epoch, not
  in total: the paymaster reopens each epoch, and $P$ paymasters buy $kP$
  proofs a node an epoch.
- Issuer memory lets anyone with $k$ honest tickets close an honest issuer
  at every node they reach for the rest of the epoch: one ticket an epoch
  kept `h0` closed 379 s of every 384 at all 30 nodes, and its spends
  waited 110 s at the median against 8 s.
- Node lists end the self-issuer's junk before the ticket check, and a node
  that lists 2 of 4 issuers carries half the honest spends, which then wait
  three times as long.

## Open questions

- Which defence, or none: the owner's choice, with these numbers.
- A node that shares what failed would close an issuer at nodes the
  attacker never reached; not modelled.

- Ask before opening the pull request from `exp/25-ticket-defences` to
  `main`, which waits on `base`. Then the descriptor base, with item 44's
  ERC-8421 descriptor.

## Learned

- A complete ruleset can still be an incoherent one. ERC-8421's modules and
  claim-gated admission each pass the completeness check alone, and
  together they loaded to a pool that ran one and ignored the other. DS-6
  now refuses the pair.
- ERC-8421's `seen` counts what a node receives by any path. Under
  claim-gated admission most honest spends reach a node inside aggregates,
  not in wrappers whose proofs it checks, so counting proof checks alone
  would have made every honest issuer look as if it failed.
- Junk is never pending, so a throttle does not bound a self-issuer; only
  the ban does, and each refresh reopens it a little.

## Open questions

- How ERC-8421's ruleset composes with dependency frames and claims. No
  clause says how per-entity budgets apply to dependency frames, or who
  keeps a ticket paymaster's reputation; DS-6 refuses the composition until
  one does.
- The surface is specified for the canonical mempool only; a node under
  ERC-8421's ruleset needs a tracer for opcodes, calls and storage
  association, and AM-15's propagation is not modelled.
- Reputation shared between nodes, which would ban a self-issuer at nodes it
  never reached, for a griefer too.
